FoundationsWhy Business Analysis Exists

Business analysis exists to close the gap between what the business needs and what gets built. Left unmanaged, that gap is where AI projects fail: a model gets shipped that technically works but solves the wrong problem, misses a regulatory constraint, or can't be explained to the executive who has to defend it. The BA discipline is the forcing function that makes intent explicit, testable, and traceable before a single line of code â€” or a single AI agent â€” is put to work.

For an AI Solution Architect, this matters more, not less, than it did for traditional software: generative AI can produce a plausible-looking requirements document, user story, or acceptance criterion in seconds. The scarce skill shifts from writing requirements to directing what gets asked for and catching what an AI-generated document gets wrong â€” silently, confidently, and often in ways that only surface after go-live.

The Core 20% You Need to Direct AI-Generated Requirements

01Functional vs. Non-Functional Requirements

Functional requirements (FR) describe what the system must do â€” a capability, a behavior, an output. Non-functional requirements (NFR) describe how well the system must do it â€” performance, security, availability, compliance, explainability. AI-generated requirement sets systematically under-produce NFRs relative to FRs, because NFRs require domain judgment about risk that a prompt rarely supplies on its own. A BRD with 20 functional requirements and zero measurable NFRs is not incomplete by accident â€” it's incomplete because nobody was forced to say how fast, how safe, or how available is "good enough." See the Business Requirements Document for a worked example of both, each with a measurable target.

02User Stories & the INVEST Test

A user story follows the shape "As a <persona>, I want <capability>, so that <benefit>." A good story is Independent, Negotiable, Valuable, Estimable, Small, and Testable. AI tends to produce stories that are Valuable-sounding but fail Testable â€” the benefit clause is vague marketing language ("so that the experience is better") rather than an outcome you could verify. When you review an AI-drafted story, ask: could two different engineers build two different things and both plausibly claim they satisfied this? If yes, it isn't testable yet.

03Acceptance Criteria â€” Given / When / Then

Acceptance criteria turn a story into a test. The Given/When/Then (Gherkin-style) format states the precondition (Given), the trigger (When), and the observable, verifiable result (Then). A story without acceptance criteria is a promise; a story with Given/When/Then is a contract a QA engineer or an AI test-generation agent can execute against. See User Stories for 15 fully worked examples, including failure-path criteria â€” not just the happy path.

04MoSCoW Prioritization

Must have (the release fails without it), Should have (important, painful to omit, but not fatal), Could have (desirable if time/budget allow), Won't have this time (explicitly descoped, and written down so nobody re-litigates it mid-sprint). The single most common defect in an AI-generated requirements set is MoSCoW collapse â€” everything is tagged "Must," which is mathematically the same as prioritizing nothing. A healthy set typically runs 40â€“60% Must, with real representation in Should/Could/Won't. Compare against the FR priority mix chart in the BRD.

05Personas

A persona is a synthesis of a real user segment's goals, decisions, and frustrations â€” not a demographic profile. "35-year-old, urban, college-educated" tells you nothing about what a person needs a system to do for them at 2am when their basement is flooding. Good personas are the lens through which you catch requirements that are technically correct but humanly useless. See Personas.

06Use Cases

Where a user story captures a slice of value, a use case captures a complete interaction end to end â€” actors, preconditions, the main success path, and critically, the alternate and exception flows: what happens when the policy is lapsed, the API times out, the user abandons mid-flow. AI-generated use cases reliably nail the main flow and just as reliably skip the unhappy paths unless explicitly directed to include them. See Use Cases.

07Traceability

Every requirement should trace forward to a design decision, a user story, a test case, and a verified status â€” and every user story and test case should trace back to a requirement. An "orphan requirement" (nothing implements it) or an "orphan feature" (something got built that no requirement asked for) is a governance failure waiting to be discovered by an auditor, not an engineer. The Requirements Traceability Matrix is the artifact that makes this checkable in one glance.

Good vs. Bad Requirements

This is the single fastest diagnostic skill to build. A requirement is testable when a stranger, reading only the requirement, could determine pass/fail without asking you a clarifying question.

Bad Requirement (as AI often drafts it)Why It FailsGood Requirement (rewritten)
"The system should be fast."No metric, no condition, unfalsifiable."The AI triage recommendation shall return within 8 seconds at the 95th percentile (NFR-020)."
"The system should be secure."Security is a property with dozens of dimensions; naming none means enforcing none."All FNOL data in transit and at rest shall be encrypted using TLS 1.2+ and AES-256 (NFR-030)."
"Users should have a good experience reporting a claim."Subjective, not testable, no owner for what "good" means."Policyholder NPS measured immediately after FNOL completion shall reach +35, up from a +12 baseline."
"The AI should detect fraud."No threshold, no action, no explainability, no human checkpoint."Claims with a fraud-indicator score ≥70 shall auto-route to the SIU queue before standard assignment, with the top 3 contributing factors shown in plain language (FR-070, NFR-050)."
"The system will mostly work offline.""Mostly" is not a state a test can assert."Intake channel shall maintain 99.9% monthly uptime, excluding scheduled maintenance windows ≤4 hours/month (NFR-010)."
Reviewer Habit

Every time you read the word "fast," "secure," "robust," "user-friendly," "scalable," or "seamless" in an AI-generated requirement with no accompanying number or condition, treat it as an unfinished requirement, not a finished one.

KPIs That Matter

A requirements set is only as good as the metrics that prove it worked. For an AI initiative, an architect should expect KPIs across four categories, all present in the ClaimsPilot AI BRD and Executive Summary:

Outcome KPIs

Did the business result change? e.g. claims leakage, policyholder NPS.

Process KPIs

Did the workflow get faster/more accurate? e.g. FNOL cycle time, straight-through routing.

Risk/Control KPIs

Is the system staying inside its guardrails? e.g. fraud model disparate-impact testing, override rates.

Adoption KPIs

Are the humans actually using it correctly? e.g. AI-suggestion override/acceptance rate, agent-channel uptake.

A KPI without a documented baseline is a slogan. Every KPI in this guide is expressed as baseline → target, never target alone.

The Architect's Review Lens

When an AI agent or a BA hands you a requirements artifact, run it through this checklist before you approve it:

1. Testability

Can every requirement be proven true or false without asking the author what they meant?

2. Priority Discipline

Is the MoSCoW mix realistic, or is everything a Must? Does the Won't list exist and get respected?

3. NFR Coverage

Are performance, security, compliance, and explainability requirements present with numbers â€” not just functional scope?

4. Failure Paths

Do use cases and stories cover what happens when a dependency fails, times out, or a human abandons the flow â€” not just the happy path?

5. Traceability

Does every requirement map to a story, a test case, and a status? Are there orphans in either direction?

6. Human Accountability

For AI-in-the-loop systems: is it unambiguous where the AI recommends and where a human decides? Is that boundary enforced in the requirements, not just implied?

7. Measurable ROI

Does the business case tie requirements to a number the CFO would recognize â€” cost, leakage, cycle time, risk exposure?

This Is the Job

You are not being trained to write the BRD. You are being trained to know, in under five minutes of reading someone else's BRD, exactly which of these seven checks it fails.

1. Purpose & Scope

This Business Requirements Document defines the scope, functional capability, and non-functional guardrails for ClaimsPilot AI, an AI-assisted First Notice of Loss (FNOL) intake and triage capability for Meridian Mutual Insurance Group's auto and homeowners lines. It is the authoritative requirements source for design, build, and test of Phases 1–3.

In scope: AI-guided FNOL intake (chat, web, mobile, voice/IVR, agent-submitted); real-time coverage verification; AI triage scoring (severity, complexity, fraud-indicator) with explainability; human adjuster/SIU review and override; structured claim packet generation into ClaimCenter.

Out of scope (this release): autonomous coverage determination or claim payment; third-party telematics integration; claims beyond FNOL (subsequent claim servicing, litigation management); commercial lines (auto/home personal lines only).

1. Problem Statement

Meridian Mutual processes ~186,000 FNOL contacts per year. Today's process is manual, phone- and form-heavy, requires data to be re-keyed up to three times before it reaches an adjuster, and routes claims by a twice-daily manual assignment batch. The result: 46–71 minute cycle times, a 29% misroute rate, $26M/year in leakage, and fraud signals that surface 3–5 days after intake rather than at the point of first contact.

2. Vision Statement

"Every Meridian policyholder receives a fast, accurate, empathetic first response to their claim â€” verified by a human, accelerated by AI â€” within minutes, not hours."

ClaimsPilot AI directly advances two pillars of Meridian's 3-year corporate plan: "Frictionless Claims Experience" (policyholder-facing cycle time and satisfaction) and "Operational Efficiency at Scale" (leakage reduction and adjuster capacity redeployment).

UC-01 â€” Submit FNOL via AI Intake Assistant

ActorsPolicyholder (primary), AI Intake Assistant, PolicyCenter
PreconditionsPolicyholder has an active policy and can authenticate.
PostconditionsA complete FNOL packet is submitted and queued for AI triage (UC-02).

Main Flow

  1. Policyholder selects "Report a New Claim."
  2. System authenticates the policyholder (policy # + last name/DOB, or portal session).
  3. System retrieves policy and coverage status from PolicyCenter.
  4. Assistant confirms active coverage and asks loss-type (auto/home).
  5. Assistant guides structured Q&A: date/time, location, description, parties, injuries, police report #.
  6. Assistant prompts for photo/video evidence upload.
  7. Assistant summarizes captured information back for confirmation.
  8. Policyholder confirms; packet submitted for AI triage (UC-02 begins).
  9. Assistant displays "what happens next" summary and estimated contact window.

Alternate Flows

  • A1 â€” Policy lapsed/cancelled: at step 3, assistant informs the policyholder, routes to a licensed agent, ends intake without collecting loss data.
  • A2 â€” Identity verification fails 3×: at step 2, hand off to a live intake specialist with partial context.
  • A3 â€” Policyholder abandons mid-flow: session inactive >10 min or tab closed → partial data saved, resumable 24 hours; reminder sent at 4 hours.
  • A4 â€” Missing required field at confirmation: at step 7, assistant re-prompts specifically for the missing field (see FR-100/US-07).

  • E1 â€” PolicyCenter unavailable: at step 3, if the API times out (>5s) or errors, the assistant offers a callback or proceeds in degraded mode, clearly flagging "coverage verification pending" for the adjuster.
  • E2 â€” Upload failure: at step 6, retries once automatically, then allows the policyholder to skip and email evidence later with a tracking reference.
Business Rules

BR-1: No loss detail is collected before coverage status is confirmed (except E1). BR-2: A packet cannot be submitted with required fields missing.

UC-02 â€” AI Triage & Routing Recommendation

ActorsAI Triage Engine (primary), Claim Packet, Claims Queue Management
PreconditionsA complete FNOL packet has been submitted (UC-01 postcondition).
PostconditionsA triage recommendation (severity, complexity, queue, fraud-indicator score + top factors) is attached and routed to the appropriate human queue.

Main Flow

  1. Engine receives the submitted packet.
  2. Engine extracts/normalizes structured fields (NLP parsing of free text as needed).
  3. Engine computes severity band, complexity score, and fraud-indicator score with top-3 contributing factors.
  4. Engine determines the recommended queue (standard adjuster pool, complex-claims unit, or SIU).
  5. Engine attaches the recommendation and explainability factors to the packet.
  6. Engine routes the packet to the recommended human queue (UC-03 begins).

Alternate Flows

  • A1 â€” Fraud-indicator ≥70: at step 4, route to SIU instead of standard queue (FR-070); standard review deferred until SIU clears or co-assigns.
  • A2 â€” Declared CAT event: at step 1, apply expedited severity-only triage to prioritize speed per the CAT playbook.

Exception Flows

  • E1 â€” Engine timeout >30s: at step 3, route to standard manual triage with a system-health alert; no AI recommendation shown (US-11 AC2).
  • E2 â€” Low-confidence extraction: at step 2, a critical field below the confidence threshold is flagged "needs human confirmation" rather than auto-populated.

UC-03 â€” Adjuster Review & Approval

ActorsClaims Adjuster / Intake Specialist (primary), AI-generated Claim Packet, ClaimCenter
PreconditionsA triage recommendation has been attached and routed to the adjuster's queue (UC-02 postcondition).
PostconditionsClaim is formally assigned in ClaimCenter with adjuster-approved data; full audit trail recorded.

Main Flow

  1. Adjuster opens the claim packet; AI-suggested fields are visually tagged.
  2. Adjuster reviews severity band, complexity score, recommended queue/next action.
  3. Adjuster reviews explainability factors for any flagged elements.
  4. Adjuster accepts fields as-is or edits any field (each edit logged with original AI value + reason code).
  5. Adjuster approves the packet.
  6. System writes the finalized claim record to ClaimCenter and formally assigns it.
  7. System logs the full decision trail (AI output, human decision, timestamps) for audit (NFR-040).

Alternate Flows

  • A1 â€” Adjuster overrides severity band: at step 4, a structured reason code is required (e.g. "photo evidence indicates higher severity"); free-text notes optional.
  • A2 â€” Adjuster requests more info: at step 2, adjuster sends the packet back to intake with a specific follow-up question; policyholder is notified and UC-01 resumes for the missing item only.

Exception Flows

  • E1 â€” ClaimCenter write failure: at step 6, retries with exponential backoff (3 attempts); if still failing, the claim queues in a dead-letter store with an adjuster/ops alert â€” never silently dropped.
  • E2 â€” Adjuster inactivity beyond DOI SLA buffer: escalates to Priya's team dashboard as an at-risk SLA item.

UC-04 â€” Fraud Indicator Escalation to SIU

ActorsSIU Fraud Analyst (primary), AI Triage Engine, Claim Packet
PreconditionsA claim has been routed to the SIU queue (fraud-indicator score ≥70, UC-02 A1).
PostconditionsSIU has made a disposition (Clear / Investigate / Refer to Legal); claim proceeds accordingly.

Main Flow

  1. Analyst opens the escalated claim from the SIU queue.
  2. Analyst reviews the fraud-indicator score, top-3 factors, and underlying data points.
  3. Analyst reviews the full claim packet and available claims history.
  4. Analyst makes a disposition: Clear, Investigate Further, or Refer to Legal.
  5. If Cleared, the claim returns to the standard adjuster queue with an SIU clearance flag. If Investigate/Refer, it enters the SIU case management workflow (outside this system's scope) and standard assignment is held.
  6. System logs the disposition and reasoning for audit and future model-feedback.

Alternate Flows

  • A1 â€” Analyst suspects model error: the case is flagged for model-quality review and included in the next quarterly fairness/accuracy audit (NFR-100/NFR-080).

Exception Flows

  • E1 â€” SIU backlog exceeds capacity threshold: alerts Denise Okafor and Priya for staffing/prioritization; claims remain held (never auto-cleared) until reviewed.
