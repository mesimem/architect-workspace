Notes
SIX POINTS. (1) THE ITINERARY-CONFLICT RULE IS ONE SENTENCE, NOT THREE CHECKS: the set of days must be exactly 1..durationDays, each appearing once. That single rule catches all three real faults - two entries claiming one day, a day past the duration, and a gap that is unsold time mid-package - and each is reported as its own named problem. Out-of-order input is VALID and gets sorted; refusing a reordered form submission is rigour that only hurts the user. (2) THE UPDATE PATH VALIDATES THE MERGED RECORD, NOT THE PATCH, AND THIS IS WHERE THE STORY ACTUALLY BITES. {durationDays: 2} on a 3-day product is a perfectly legal patch in isolation; applied, it leaves the stored itinerary describing a day that no longer exists. Same fault from the other side: replacing the itinerary alone can leave a gap against the stored duration. Neither is reachable now, and both have named regression cases. (3) THE AUDIT ENTRY CARRIES AN ITINERARY DIGEST, NOT THE ITINERARY, AND THAT IS DELIBERATE. auditLog.js caps context nesting at MAX_CONTEXT_DEPTH and replaces anything deeper with "<truncated>". A before/after pair holding full day objects sits below that cap, so writing it would produce an entry that LOOKS complete and actually reads "<truncated>" where the evidence should be. {days: 3} plus daysChanged: [2] is smaller, true, and answers the question an auditor asks first - which day moved - when a customer says their day 4 differs from the brochure. Recording less beats recording something that silently becomes "<truncated>". (4) THE ONE CHANGE OUTSIDE THIS STORY WAS APPROVED BY THE OPERATOR FIRST, and it TIGHTENS a test rather than loosening one. permissions.test.js:127 asserts the exact role list, so the fifth role had to be added there. More importantly, its CRM block's own comment warned that "a loop over ROLES would silently pass the day a fifth role is added with CRM access" - that fifth role is now us, so product_manager is asserted by hand to hold no CRM permission. Left alone, that suite would have stayed green while its stated claim, "the CRM is reachable by sales alone", quietly stopped being proven. (5) A NEW ROLE RATHER THAN A WIDER ADVISOR, chosen by the operator against the cheaper alternative. Giving the product grants to advisor was one fewer row in the table in exchange for every advisor being able to reprice any package the agency sells. An advisor holds products.read - sell from the book, do not author it - and that read/write split is the entire reason there are two permissions. product_manager holds no CRM, no audit read, no quotes and no proposals. (6) SCOPE, AND WHAT WAS DELIBERATELY NOT BUILT. costPerPersonCents is REQUIRED, because without it there is no margin and the below-cost refusal - the check that catches the $5,200 written as 5200 cents - can never fire; cost and margin are nested under pricing.internal so a future customer-facing projection has exactly one field to filter, which is also why products.read is staff-only today. PRICING IS REPLACED AS A BLOCK, not merged field by field, so a patch omitting singleSupplementCents drops it to 0 - asserted explicitly so the behaviour is a decision rather than a discovery. No seasonal or per-date pricing (the backend deliberately holds no rate card - STORY-007's reasoning, and a quote carries the season), no per-traveller tiers, no link between a product and the africa/catalogSource.js destination it visits, no DELETE route (an unsold package is a status question, and erasing a product erases the subject of its own audit trail), no pagination (STORY-016 owns scale - list and the duplicate check both scan, O(n) on purpose). NO DURABILITY SUITE YET: the store uses the same jsonFileStore as every other store here and inherits its behaviour, but unlike STORY-007/008/013 that has not been proven for THIS module in separate processes, and it is the most valuable thing to add next. version is recorded but NOT enforced - two product managers editing at once is last-write-wins, the standing repo-wide limit, and the real fix is Postgres.
